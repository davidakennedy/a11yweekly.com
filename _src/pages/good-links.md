title: What Makes a Good Link?
description: Guidance for what types of content work well in Accessibility Weekly.
permalink: /good-links/
---

Accessibility Weekly aims to deliver a fast and informative read every week, helping people interested in accessibility learn more. To do that, I curate links thoughtfully. What makes a good link for the newsletter, you might ask?

A good link:

- **includes a recent publish date**. I prefer content published within the last two months, if possible. Older content with timeless information can be useful for the "New to A11y (Accessibility)" section.
- **brings accuracy**. Accessibility comes with enough nuance already since many of the answers are "It depends." The information should be as correct as possible.
- **provides a perspective**. Good writing tells a story and has a point of view. The best links aren't just tutorials with the facts, but contain details about how you learned those facts and how it changed you or your organization.
- **helps people learn about digital accessibility**. I created the newsletter to promote learning about accessibility. The better a link does this, the more chance it has to make it in.
- **features the lived experience of people with disabilities**. I try to elevate the work and experiences of people with disabilities whenever I can. It's the best way for accessibility practitioners to think critically about how those with disabilities participate in accessibility work.
- **lacks a paywall, email sign-up form, or a press release**. You can build your customer base or email list another way. I also avoid links with strong call to actions at the end, directing people to paid products. The newsletter sometimes links to industry products with substantial new features that have a free version or tier. I include press releases rarely, and try to avoid them. I'd rather read a blog post that dives deeper into the how of the press release subject.
- **isn't just a survey or data without context and/or value.** Some organizations run surveys or studies, and release the results to build publicity, email lists, or potential customer bases. I'm choosey about which ones I feature, and don't often include them unless they bring valuable insight.
- **isn't a job listing**. Job links expire, making it hard to know when that happens. Job boards like [A11y Jobs](https://www.a11yjobs.com) provide a better venue than a newsletter.
- **never promotes accessibility overlays**. [What's an overlay](https://overlayfactsheet.com/#what-is-a-web-accessibility-overlay)? Overlays don't solve an accessibility problem and make the experience worse for disabled people. I don't recommend them ([many in the industry don't](https://shouldiuseanaccessibilityoverlay.com), and won't promote them or any work by an overlay company.
- **lives on a domain that isn't social media**. I'll occasionally link to social media posts if it's newsworthy. However, I try to avoid it since these sites tend to break links, go out of business, etc. over time. I want to link to content that has a better chance of lasting. Related note: I avoid linking to Medium.com posts now since many aren't readable without an account.
- **doesn't link directly to an app in an app store**. Linking to apps implies an endorsement in many ways. I don't have the time to test apps, nor am I often the primary audience. Instead, write a blog post or create a video/talk about the why behind the app and/or how it took an inclusive approach to its design.
- **goes deeper than a product page or tool**. Linking to a product page or tool, much like apps, implies an endorsement. I don't have the time to test products fully, nor am I often the primary audience most of the time. Instead, write a blog post or create a video/talk about the why behind the app/tool and/or how it took an inclusive approach to its design. I sometimes link to small, focused tools that do one thing well, and help practitioners judge the accessibility of a site, app, or product better.
- **has a human behind it**. While AI (artificial intelligence) will continue to evolve, and play a role in technology and accessibility, having a human in the loop remains critical. If your blog post, tool or resource includes AI, explain how you used it, and how it helps with accessibility.

A good link doesn't have to hit all of these points, of course, but it's a good start.

Lastly, Accessibility Weekly doesn't participate in link exchanges, guest posts or the purchase of email lists.
